Studies: a setup tree in the style of COMSOL with studies that hold their own solver and timing, parametric sweeps over part position and angle run as one study through a queue, a reworked main window, and two wall fixes in the solver found by the first sweeps. Wing results change with this release (see Validation).

## Highlights

- **Studies:** the tree now has Case (physics and geometry), Study and Results branches. A study is one way of solving the case, with its own solver and timing settings; several studies per project. Run (F5) runs the selected study, and Run again replaces its earlier results.
- **Parametric sweeps:** add a sweep below a study, pick a part, the parameter (position or angle) and the values (a list, or start, end and count), and the runs queue one after another. The study's Results node plots C_x and C_y against the swept value, with the standard error.
- **Queue:** the Queue tab shows the runs waiting and the one running; waiting runs can be removed or reordered, and Pause holds the queue after the current run. Stop ends the run and the rest of its study.
- **Main window:** tree and settings on the left; the case, a run's coefficients or a sweep plot on the right above the Summary, Console and Queue tabs. Stop needs two clicks, on the ribbon or beside the progress bar. A part shows only the settings of its kind.
- **Resolution warning:** a part whose tightest curve (a NACA leading edge, or the smallest smooth radius of an STL) is under 5 cells is flagged, because its forces then depend on where it sits within a cell. The manual's Reference explains the limit.

## Solver fixes

- **Links across periodic boundaries:** wall links that crossed a periodic face (the span of the quasi-2D cases) were dropped, about 10% of the wing's links. They now wrap like the flow does.
- **Edges thinner than a cell:** a trailing edge thinner than a cell had no grid points inside it and was invisible to the walls. Links that cross such an edge now carry a wall from both sides.
- The wing template's lift rises by about 18% with both fixes. The sphere, Ahmed body and spinning cylinder are unchanged or within their standard error.

## Fixes

- An Ahmed body too large for the domain is refused with the size that does not fit, instead of crashing the solver.
- Plot axes no longer scale their numbers by a hidden factor (x0.001).
- An edit a form refuses now says why in the status bar.

## Requirements

- Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.
- NVIDIA GPU with a current driver (CUDA).
- About 1.5 GB of disk space.

## Install

1. Download `HomebrewCFD-0.4.0-setup.exe` and check it, either against `SHA256SUMS.txt`:
   `Get-FileHash HomebrewCFD-0.4.0-setup.exe -Algorithm SHA256`
   or with the attestation:
   `gh attestation verify HomebrewCFD-0.4.0-setup.exe --repo JDavies101/Homebrew-CFD`
2. Run the installer. The Windows prompt shows **Jake Davies** as the verified publisher. SmartScreen may still warn until the certificate builds reputation; check the publisher, then choose **More info → Run anyway**.
3. Start Homebrew CFD. On the start page, double-click **wing ground** under Templates, Save as a project and press **F5**. Help > Manual (F1) walks through it, including a ride-height sweep.

## Known limitations

- Results are trustworthy for relative comparisons and trends, not as certification-grade absolute coefficients.
- Forces depend on sub-cell placement by a few percent while a curved edge is under about 5 cells in radius (the wing template's leading edge is 1.3 cells). Local grid refinement is planned.
- Parametric sweeps cover part position and angle; flow and mesh parameters come later.
- The Ahmed body has staircase walls only; imported STLs cannot yet be rotated in the app.
- Windows and NVIDIA (CUDA) only.
